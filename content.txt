     1	# KOYA BANK — Comprehensive MVP North-Star Plan
     2	
     3	## TL;DR
     4	
     5	Koya Bank is a borderless financial operating system targeting African global workers and crypto-natives. The MVP delivers a full-stack fintech platform: multi-currency wallets (KES/USD/BTC/USDC/USDT), universal asset conversion, M-Pesa rails, stablecoin USD accounts, US stock trading, card issuance, and a transactional WhatsApp bot — all wrapped in a premium glassmorphism UI. Built solo on NestJS + Next.js + PostgreSQL, deployed on AWS, with a 3-month aggressive timeline requiring strict phased milestones.
     6	
     7	**Competitive edge over Xapo:** Xapo is Bitcoin-centric, members-only, Gibraltar-regulated, targets HNW Bitcoin holders. Koya is Africa-first, multi-asset, accessibility-focused (WhatsApp bot, guest swaps, M-Pesa integration), and serves the underbanked global worker — not just Bitcoin maximalists.

   225	### Step 7: Conversion/FX Engine (Days 15–22) *depends on Step 6*
   226	
   227	**The conversion engine is Koya's core product differentiator.**
   228	
   229	#### Supported Pairs (full matrix):
   230	```
   231	KES ↔ BTC    KES ↔ USD    KES ↔ USDT   KES ↔ USDC
   232	BTC ↔ USD    BTC ↔ USDT   BTC ↔ USDC
   233	USDT ↔ USD   USDC ↔ USD   USDT ↔ USDC
   234	```
   235	
   236	#### Quote Engine:
   237	- Fetches real-time prices from multiple sources
   238	- Applies Koya spread (revenue): 0.5% – 1.5% depending on pair and volume
   239	- Quote valid for 30 seconds (configurable)
   240	- Prices cached in Redis (5 second TTL for volatile pairs, 30 sec for stablecoin/stablecoin)
   241	
   242	#### Liquidity Strategy (CRITICAL for solo operator):
   243	
   244	| Pair Category | Recommended Source | Backup |
   245	|--------------|-------------------|--------|
   246	| BTC ↔ USD/USDT/USDC | Binance Spot API | Kraken API |
   247	| USDT ↔ USDC | 1:1 with tiny spread (internal) | Binance |
   248	| USDC ↔ USD | 1:1 via stablecoin custodian/Circle | Internal |
   249	| KES ↔ USD | Sponsor bank FX / Flutterwave | TransferZero |
   250	| KES ↔ BTC | Route through KES→USD→BTC | Yellowcard API |
   251	| KES ↔ USDT/USDC | Route through KES→USD→stablecoin | P2P matching |
   252	
   253	**Key insight:** Most KES pairs should be routed through USD as intermediary. You don't need 10 direct liquidity pools — you need 3 (KES↔USD, BTC↔USD, stablecoin↔USD) and route everything else through these.
   254	
   255	#### Execution Flow:
   256	1. User requests quote (source currency, target currency, amount)
   257	2. Quote engine fetches best price, adds spread, returns quote with `quote_id`
   258	3. User accepts quote within TTL
   259	4. Execution engine:  
   260	   a. Lock source funds (wallet.available_balance -= amount)  
   261	   b. Execute against liquidity source (Binance trade, FX transfer, etc.)  
   262	   c. On success: Create ledger entries (debit source wallet, credit target wallet, credit fee account)  
   263	   d. On failure: Unlock funds, mark transaction failed  
   264	5. Send confirmation notification

   670	## Decisions & Assumptions
   671	
   672	1. **Monorepo over microservices:** As a solo developer, a NestJS monorepo with well-separated modules is faster than managing 10 microservices. The module boundaries are designed so you can extract to microservices later when you have a team.
   673	
   674	2. **Prisma over TypeORM:** Prisma gives you type-safe database access, automatic migrations, and a great developer experience. Critical for a solo dev moving fast.
   675	
   676	3. **Liquidity routing through USD:** Instead of maintaining 10+ direct liquidity pools, route most conversions through USD as the intermediate currency. This reduces complexity from O(n²) liquidity pools to O(n).
   677	
   678	4. **No own banking license initially:** Operate under a sponsor bank model (or as a technology provider). Engage CBK for licensing path in parallel with building. This is the standard approach (Xapo started with Bitcoin custody, added banking license later through Gibraltar).
   679	
   680	5. **Fireblocks for crypto custody:** While expensive, Fireblocks provides MPC wallets, policy engine, and regulatory compliance out of the box. For MVP, you can start with a simpler setup (e.g., BitGo or even self-custody with proper key management) and upgrade.
   681	
   682	6. **React Native over native iOS/Android:** As a solo developer, code reuse is essential. Expo + React Native gives you both platforms with ~90% shared code.
   683	
   684	7. **Guest swaps as growth wedge:** This is your user acquisition funnel. No sign-up friction → demonstrate value → convert to full accounts. Xapo requires membership application — Koya's openness is a competitive advantage.
   685	
   686	8. **WhatsApp as distribution channel:** 97% of Kenyans with smartphones use WhatsApp. A full transactional bot isn't just a feature — it's market access to users who won't download another app.

   690	## Critical Risk Factors
   691	
   692	1. **Regulatory:** Operating without a license is the #1 risk. Mitigate: Engage a Kenyan fintech lawyer immediately. Consider launching under a sandbox/pilot license from CBK Innovation Hub. Research if you can operate as a "technology platform" with a licensed partner.
   693	
   694	2. **Liquidity:** You need funded exchange accounts with sufficient BTC, USDC, USDT, and KES liquidity before launch. Under-capitalized liquidity = failed conversions = dead product.
   695	
   696	3. **Solo developer risk:** This plan is executable but demanding. Prioritize ruthlessly: Launch with guest swaps + web wallets first, then layer features. Don't try to ship everything on Day 60.
   697	
   698	4. **M-Pesa production access:** Getting Daraja production credentials requires a registered Kenyan business entity and compliance review. Start the application process in Week 1.
